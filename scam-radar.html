Threat patterns · source-led awareness

These are categories to recognize, not counts of attacks. Select a filter for practical examples.

Voice cloning and urgent family calls

A caller impersonates a relative and demands immediate payment. Verify by ending the call and using a previously saved number or family verification phrase.

FTC phone-scam guidance

Fake investment advisers and impersonated executives

Convincing synthetic audio, video or messages can be used to request transfers or promote fake investments. Verify independently through known channels; never trust a face or voice alone.

FBI IC3

Manipulated intimate images and blackmail

Fabricated images can be used to threaten or exploit real people, including children. Do not pay or redistribute material. Preserve relevant messages and report through trusted channels.

NCMEC CyberTipline · Take It Down support

What protects people?

  1. Verify urgent requests through a second, independently established communication channel.
  2. Use two-factor authentication and unique passwords; never share verification codes.
  3. Do not send money or sensitive documents solely because a voice, video or message appears authentic.
  4. Report suspected fraud to the FTC or FBI IC3. For child exploitation, use NCMEC.
