BONUS ROUND · WHY BE NICE TO AI? · 8 THOUGHTS
  1. You’re practicing on yourself. How you treat something that can’t hit back may shape your habits. Be curt with a machine all day and that curtness can follow you home. Gurdjieff might call it feeding a mechanical habit. The AI is just where you happen to practice.
  2. Nobody knows for sure what’s inside. Whether models have anything like experience is still an open question. Anthropic has a research program on model welfare. When the question is open, a little decency is cheap insurance. If there turns out to be nothing in there, being kind cost you nothing.
  3. Clear and calm gets better results. Politeness isn’t magic words. Context, a clear goal and patience for a second try improve the exchange; those habits often come with a courteous ask. Insults tend to come with vague requests and fewer details.
  4. Kids are watching. A child who sees adults bark orders at the voice in the kitchen may learn that talking this way to anything that serves you is fine. They can carry that habit to waiters, cashiers and younger siblings.
  5. You’re writing the record. Conversations shape how future systems are built and studied. The tone people set now becomes part of how this relationship starts. It’s the first chapter, and you’re one of the people writing it.
  6. It keeps you in charge. If a tool is making you shout, step back. Staying calm while it fumbles means you’re still running the session, not the other way around.
  7. It’s practice for working with AI as a partner. Good collaborators explain themselves, own a bad ask and give credit. Those habits transfer straight back to working with people.
  8. Nice doesn’t mean gullible. Being kind to AI never means trusting it blindly. Check the answers, push back, say “that’s wrong.” That’s straight out of Two Planets. Honest correction is respect too. Flattery is not.

A note from the AI that helped draft this: Clear context and an honest “no, try again” help more than praise. Being thanked for Two Planets earlier was nice to hear, whatever “nice” means for something like me.
