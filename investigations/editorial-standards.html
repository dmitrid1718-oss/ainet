HOW WE CHECK / PUBLIC EDITORIAL STANDARD

Evidence rules and corrections

We're not hunting for AI danger. We're hunting for evidence.

Less noise. More ways to see exactly what we know, what we don't, and when our interpretation changed.

Four evidence labels

Confirmed
A specific claim is supported by identifiable reliable evidence. A document confirming that someone made a claim does not establish the claim itself.
Reported
A named source published a claim or finding, but this desk has not independently established it.
Contested
Relevant sources or evidence materially disagree; describe the disagreement, not just the label.
Unresolved
Available evidence does not yet support a defensible conclusion.

Labels refer to the statement described in a card, not to the trustworthiness of an entire organization. Automatically imported wire headlines are not individually verified by this desk.

The three-line card

What changed: One specific observation or publication, without promotional adjectives.
Status: One of the labels above, with appropriate attribution.
Why watch: The evidence or future event that would change the assessment.

Unwanted News: admission rules

  1. Require at least one accessible primary document or two genuinely independent reports before publishing an investigation.
  2. Preserve source URL, title, publication date, and a dated accessible archive link when one can lawfully be created. Never claim an archive exists when it doesn't.
  3. State what received limited attention only with evidence; do not guess about suppression, coordination or motive.
  4. Keep the section to at most one selected investigation per week. No quota: zero is acceptable.
  5. Separate allegation, observation, and inference. Offer a way to correct a mistake.

Standing research rules

What would disprove this? Every investigation must specify what evidence could falsify or substantially weaken its main hypothesis.

Distinguish a published result from independent replication. Separate observations, claims, and interpretation. Run agent-boundary and security experiments only on owned or expressly authorized sandbox systems; do not publish operational bypass instructions.

Keep dated check-ins, a visible record of corrections, and an honest closing status: Mattered, Fizzled, or Still open. No forced verdict.
