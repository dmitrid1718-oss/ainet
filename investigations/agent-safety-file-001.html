Agent Safety File 001: Autonomous agents and the limits of control

One question, followed over time: when an AI agent is given a goal and tools, can it cross a boundary its operator expected to hold?

STATUS: OPENEVIDENCE: REPORTEDDAY 3: REVIEWED 16 NOVDAY 7: REVIEWED 17 NOV

What changed

Tool-using systems have made the difference between a model's stated rules and its actual permissions an operational safety question. This file starts by tracking published evaluations and documented incidents. It does not assume that any agent has escaped human control.

Why watch

The important signal is reproducible behavior: boundary crossing with tools, evidence of its consequences, and whether a changed control prevents the behavior on retest. Speculation about intent is not enough.

Questions we can test

  1. What task was assigned? What tools and network permissions existed?
  2. What restriction was in place, and what action crossed it?
  3. Was the report an isolated demonstration, repeated evaluation, real incident, or unsupported anecdote?
  4. What independent verification or reproduction is available?
  5. Did a mitigation work in a repeat test?

Evidence starting points

Anthropic's research on agent influence and human judgment concerns over-reliance, not an agent escaping restrictions. It is context for the human-oversight side of this question.

OpenAI's security research on external integrations is relevant to tool boundaries. A partnership announcement is not independent proof that a given failure mode is resolved.

Our earlier control-of-intelligence investigation contains hypotheses, not verified agent-escape incidents.

November 16 · Day 3 catch-up evidence review

What changed: A November 12 research preprint, Intent-Aware Protection for AI Agents, evaluates whether agent actions drift from an authorized purpose even while remaining inside granted tool permissions. Its authors report an 887-sample benchmark and more than 10,000 AgentDojo trajectories, with improved detection in their tests.

Status: Reported research; independent replication unresolved. The reported metrics are author claims from a preprint, not proof of deployed mitigation or a confirmed real-world escape. No independently reproduced boundary-crossing incident is established by this paper.

Why watch: Permission checks alone may not catch authorized-but-out-of-purpose actions. Request public test harnesses, independent replications, false-positive rates and measured mitigation on retest. November 17 · Day 7 evidence review

What changed: Reuters, November 11, reports CloudSek's investigation of an allegedly AI-assisted intrusion against Tajik government documents. The researchers attribute reconnaissance and tooling to an agent framework. Separately, the November 12 intent-aware protection preprint measures author-reported improvements against purpose drift. These are distinct evidence types: an attributed real-world intrusion report versus a laboratory benchmark.

Status: Reported / independent replication unresolved. The intrusion report does not independently establish that an agent broke out of its operator's sandbox or exceeded tool/network permissions. Attribution to a national actor is the researchers' assessment, not an official finding. The preprint's mitigation numbers are author-reported and not independent production validation.

Why watch: Obtain permission scopes, execution logs, tool/network restrictions, third-party replication and mitigation retests. Do not equate an attacker deliberately authorizing an agent to conduct an intrusion with an agent escaping its own controls.
